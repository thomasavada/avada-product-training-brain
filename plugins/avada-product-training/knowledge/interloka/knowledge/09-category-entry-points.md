# Category Entry Points (CEP)

**Source anchor:** `IMG_7166.HEIC`

## Definition

Category Entry Points are cues buyers use to access category and brand memories in a buying situation.

```text
Cue / situation arises
→ category comes to mind
→ a small set of brands is recalled
→ those brands enter consideration
```

A brand cannot be chosen if it is not remembered at the moment the category becomes relevant.

## Source-native 6W generation framework

- **WHY?** Why are they buying from the category?
- **WHEN?** When does the need arise?
- **WHERE?** Where does the buying/usage situation happen?
- **WITH WHOM?** Who are they with?
- **WITH WHAT?** What adjacent activity, product, food, tool, or context is involved?
- **FOR WHOM?** Who is the purchase/use intended for?

## Do not confuse

| Concept | Question |
|---|---|
| Segment / ICP | Which group/account is strategically attractive? |
| Persona | What role/context/influence does a person have? |
| JTBD | What progress are they trying to make? |
| CEP | Which cue/situation makes the category and brands come to mind? |
| Category Positioning | What category/strategic industry does the brand claim? |
| POD | Why prefer this brand once considered? |
| Funnel stage | Where are they in the company's conversion measurement? |

## CEP generation

1. Define the category precisely.
2. Collect real buying/usage episodes.
3. Code WHY/WHEN/WHERE/WITH WHOM/WITH WHAT/FOR WHOM.
4. Group recurring cues without making them too broad.
5. Estimate frequency and reach.
6. Measure which brands are recalled for each cue.
7. Prioritize CEPs the brand can credibly build.
8. Design product, distribution, distinctive assets, content, and proof around the chosen CEPs.

## CEP quality

A useful CEP is:

- specific enough to trigger a situation;
- broad enough to occur repeatedly across buyers;
- category-linked, not merely a brand message;
- based on evidence rather than brainstorm alone;
- actionable through product, distribution, media, and memory assets.

## Priority criteria

- Frequency of the situation.
- Reach across category buyers.
- Category relevance.
- Brand fit/permission.
- Competitive crowding.
- Economic value.
- Ability to activate through channels and experience.

## Relationship to Category Positioning

Category Positioning defines **what the brand is**. CEPs define **when/why that category and brand should be remembered**.

A single Category Positioning can be linked to multiple CEPs. Growth often comes from building more relevant memory links—not endlessly rewriting the positioning statement.
