# Section 1 — Strategy & Business Model

**Source range:** `IMG_7124`–`IMG_7162`, with formal section start visible in `IMG_7134`.

## [SOURCE] Role in the integrated framework

The course begins with the enterprise as a coordinated system:

- resources: people, technology, finance, data, marketing, culture, partnerships;
- leadership thinking and capability;
- Job-to-be-Done;
- business model;
- competitive strategy;
- product R&D;
- brand strategy;
- culture;
- Big Why and Strategy Goals.

## [SOURCE] Three value questions of a business model

`IMG_7137` frames a good business model through:

1. **Value Created** — what value is created and for whom?
2. **Value Delivered** — how the enterprise organizes the journey/system to deliver it.
3. **Value Captured** — how the enterprise captures value/revenue/profit.

## [SOURCE] Business Model Matrix

`IMG_7147` and `IMG_7152` connect:

### Operation Formula

- Key resources: people, technology, finance, data, marketing, culture, external partnerships.
- Operation formula: how resources are coordinated to maximize value and minimize cost.
- Pattern/know-how: can the operating formula be named, repeated, and protected as a distinctive know-how?

### Customer Value Proposition

- product/service value;
- differentiation in product/service quality;
- differentiation in how value is delivered to customers;
- relation to the Job-to-be-Done and Product–Market Fit.

### Profit Formula

- revenue formula;
- value-chain participation: which stages are owned vs partnered;
- cost priorities across category competing factors;
- value curve and willingness to invest differently from competitors.

## [SOURCE] Product–Market Fit

`IMG_7141` asks whether someone truly has the job/need and whether the proposed value is viable before scaling a full model.

## [SYNTHESIS] Preferred global teaching cases

- **Airbnb:** a real supply/demand job required trust, reviews, payments, and marketplace liquidity—not merely a listing website.
- **Dropbox/Spotify:** freemium only works when free usage creates distribution and the paid tier retains meaningful upgrade value.
- **Dell:** build-to-order ties customer value to inventory and working-capital economics.

## [SOURCE] Competitive strategy and value curve

`IMG_7148`–`IMG_7153` show that a similar business model can still produce a different competitive strategy; different models can also imply different curves. For teaching, use the global cases in `case-studies/global/business-model-patterns-global.md`.

## [SOURCE] Business-model innovation

`IMG_7144`–`IMG_7162` cover:

- white space for a new business model;
- focus on the customer value proposition;
- break the status quo;
- business-model innovation and repositioning;
- 7-Eleven and Anna eyewear cases;
- translating a changed operating model into a new positioning and customer experience.

## [SYNTHESIS] Learning questions

- What job and value justify the enterprise?
- What resource/operation system produces that value?
- Which value-chain stages should be owned or partnered?
- What revenue/cost logic makes the system viable?
- Which parts are generic pattern vs proprietary know-how?
- How does business-model change force repositioning?
