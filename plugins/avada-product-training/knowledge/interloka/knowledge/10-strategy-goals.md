# Strategy Goals — From Big Why to Capability Staircase

**Source anchor:** `IMG_7127.HEIC`

## Distinctions

- **Big Why:** enduring reason for existence/change.
- **Strategy Goal:** desired strategic future state or position.
- **Strategic Choices:** what the enterprise will and will not do.
- **Capability Staircase:** intermediate capabilities/products needed to reach the future state.
- **Milestones/KPIs:** evidence the enterprise is moving in the intended direction.

A financial target alone is not a strategy goal. Revenue is usually an outcome of position, value, capability, and execution.

## Sony 25-Year BHAG

> Become the company most known for changing the worldwide image of Japanese products as being of poor quality.

Why it works as a strategic goal:

- long horizon;
- clear desired perception shift;
- competitive meaning beyond revenue;
- requires sustained product quality, engineering, proof, and brand building;
- informs what Sony must become, not only what projects to launch.

## Apple 1983 strategy example

Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vk3vo7xVkJc

Desired future state:

> An incredibly great computer in a book, portable, learnable in 20 minutes.

Strategic logic:

1. Acknowledge the end-state is technically impossible now.
2. Reject doing nothing.
3. Reject shipping a poor-quality portable computer because competitors are doing it.
4. Design toward the desired future computer before the final form factor is possible.
5. Build a capability staircase:
   - bread-box form / ~$10,000 (Lisa);
   - shoe-box form / ~$2,500;
   - book form / under $1,000.

The example combines a clear future product, customer value, explicit NOs, staged capability building, and price/form-factor milestones.

## Strategy Goal Canvas

1. Big Why.
2. Current strategic position.
3. Desired future state.
4. Horizon.
5. Strategic choices.
6. Explicit NOs.
7. Capability staircase.
8. Milestones/KPIs.
9. Falsification criteria.

## Quality test

A strategy goal should help the team answer:

- What future are we building toward?
- Why does that future matter?
- What will we refuse to do?
- Which capabilities must be accumulated in sequence?
- Which intermediate product/market steps are acceptable?
- What evidence would show the direction is wrong?
