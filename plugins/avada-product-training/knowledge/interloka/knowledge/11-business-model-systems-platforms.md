# Business Model as a System — Edison, Apple, Shopify

## Product is not the whole Business Model

A product is the visible artifact. A Business Model explains the system that makes the product useful, scalable, defensible, and economically viable:

```text
Product
+ infrastructure
+ participants
+ activities/rules
+ incentives
+ distribution/trust
+ money/value flows
= Business Model system
```

## Thomas Edison — electricity system

The strategic achievement was not “inventing electricity” or simply “inventing the bulb.” The commercial system connected:

- generation/dynamos and central stations;
- transmission/distribution wiring;
- bulbs and electrical devices;
- installation and maintenance;
- standards and safety;
- meters/measurement;
- financing and commercial organization;
- recurring usage economics.

The bulb had limited value without the ecosystem enabling reliable usage and payment.

## Apple — hardware + OS + App Store ecosystem

Participants:

- Apple: hardware, OS, SDK, App Store governance, review, billing, distribution;
- developers: apps, innovation, niche use cases;
- users: installed base, attention, demand, data, payment.

Value flow:

```text
More useful apps
→ devices become more valuable
→ more users / installed base
→ stronger developer incentive
→ more apps and investment
```

Value capture includes hardware margin and services/platform economics. The App Store is a key ecosystem layer, not the whole Apple Business Model.

## Shopify — commerce platform + App Store ecosystem

Participants:

- Shopify: core commerce platform, APIs, governance, App Store, billing;
- developers/agencies: apps, themes, services, integrations;
- merchants: demand, subscriptions, transaction volume, feedback;
- buyers: traffic, orders, data, repeat behavior.

The App Store enables Shopify to offer broad/vertical capability without building every feature itself. Value capture includes subscription, merchant solutions, ecosystem revenue, and indirect retention/platform attractiveness.

## Platform economics

Direct economics:

```text
Active participants
× attach rate / transactions
× ARPU / GMV
× take rate
− platform variable/governance cost
= direct contribution
```

Indirect economics:

- higher core-product demand;
- retention/switching cost;
- product breadth and faster innovation;
- developer/supplier investment;
- data/learning;
- distribution advantage.

## Key caveat

An App Store or marketplace does not automatically create a healthy network effect. It must produce real cross-side value:

- more apps/partners improve customer/merchant outcomes;
- more users/merchants improve developer/supplier opportunity;
- quality/governance costs remain controlled;
- the ecosystem does not become confusing, low-quality, or extractive.
