# C-D Map — Centrality × Distinctiveness

## Purpose

The C-D Map helps discuss how a brand balances:

- **Centrality:** how representative/mainstream the brand is in the chosen category; how broadly it enters consideration.
- **Distinctiveness:** how strongly it stands apart in buyer memory and meaning.

It is most useful for exploring strategic direction, portfolio roles, pricing power, and the risk of becoming generic or too niche.

## Four discussion quadrants

| Quadrant | Meaning |
|---|---|
| High centrality / high distinctiveness | Broadly considered and meaningfully different; strong leading position |
| High centrality / low distinctiveness | Mainstream and accessible but easier to substitute |
| Low centrality / high distinctiveness | Boutique/niche/premium; strong meaning with narrower reach |
| Low centrality / low distinctiveness | Peripheral; weak recall and weak differentiation |

## Automotive workshop

Use automotive because learners can intuitively debate brand meaning and reach.

Hypotheses to research—not fixed scores:

- **Toyota/Ford:** central/mainstream candidates.
- **BMW/Mercedes-Benz:** candidates for high centrality plus strong distinctiveness in premium cars.
- **Porsche/Ferrari:** highly distinctive boutique candidates with selective reach.
- **Tesla:** its position changes depending on whether the category is cars, EVs, or technology-led mobility—and by market/date.
- **Undifferentiated new entrant:** peripheral candidate.

## Measurement discipline

Before placing brands:

1. define category, country, audience, and observation date;
2. measure awareness/consideration/usage for centrality;
3. measure strength and uniqueness of associations for distinctiveness;
4. use a separate bubble size for scale/revenue/volume;
5. publish scoring rules and confidence;
6. call unsupported positions workshop judgment, not data.

## Strategic movement

- Moving **right** usually means increasing salience, distribution, category representativeness, and broad consideration.
- Moving **up** usually means strengthening unique associations, experience, assets, and meaningful difference.
- Moving premium/niche can increase distinctiveness while reducing centrality.
- A brand can lose distinctiveness as it scales if its meaning becomes generic.
