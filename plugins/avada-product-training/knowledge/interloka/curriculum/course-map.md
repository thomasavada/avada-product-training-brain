# Course Map

## Source-aligned teaching architecture

The course follows the photographed Interloka sequence. It teaches foundational strategic marketing through the course's own concepts; it does not prepend a separate SaaS/growth-marketing syllabus.

```text
OPENING FOUNDATION — IMG_7124–7133
enterprise system → Big Why → Strategy Goals → research
→ C-D Map → strategic segment → Business Model Spectrum

SIX INTERLOKA SECTIONS — IMG_7134–7210
1. Strategy & Business Model
2. Category Positioning
3. Functional Differentiators
4. Brand Archetype
5. Core Values & Corporate Culture
6. Positioning Big Idea

FINAL CAPSTONE
Business Model in One Page + evidence appendix + executive defense
```

Foundation doctrine: `curriculum/interloka-foundations.md`.

## Suggested delivery

### Phase 0 — Interloka opening foundation

- Enterprise as one system.
- Big Why and Strategy Goals.
- Research before Where to Play.
- C-D Map.
- Business Model Spectrum: Selective, Sandwich, Big Mass.
- Strategic-segment foundation brief.

### Phase A — Section 1: Strategy & Business Model

- Value Created / Delivered / Captured.
- Business Model Matrix.
- Key resources and Operation Formula.
- Revenue/Profit Formula and value-chain choices.
- Product–Market Fit and JTBD.
- Competing factors and Strategy Canvas.
- Blue Ocean and Value Innovation.
- Business-model innovation/repositioning.

### Phase B — Sections 2–6: Integrated Brand Strategy

- Category positioning and CEPs.
- Functional differentiation/POD/RTB.
- Distinctiveness and brand equity.
- Archetype/personality.
- Culture/core values.
- Positioning Big Idea.

### Phase C — Final synthesis

- Complete the consulting templates.
- Build Business Model in One Page.
- Attach evidence, assumptions, and unresolved questions.
- Present and defend coherence from resources/economics through brand/culture.

## Course outcome

Learners can explain and defend:

- where the enterprise chooses to play and why;
- what value the business creates/delivers/captures;
- which operation/profit model supports it;
- which competing factors receive more/less investment;
- what category the brand should own;
- what functional and emotional differences are credible;
- which culture can execute the strategy;
- which Big Idea compresses the whole system.
